# Below the Pass: Chapter 1 Script

Snapshot of the Claude Doc "Below the Pass: Chapter 1 Script", 2026-11-14. If the doc changes, export it again over this file.


## Premise and setting

**Winter, 1986. A 26-year-old detective nobody takes seriously is sent on an errand over the mountains, gets snowed into a small town, and lands the kind of case his boss swore he could never handle.**

- **The town:** Northvale, an old silver-mining town below the Harrow Pass. One railway line over the mountain, one hotel, one café, one policeman. Rotary phones, a payphone at the station, electric clocks. No computers anywhere.
- **The storm:** the forecast promised a clear night. Within an hour the pass is buried. The last train of the night stops at Northvale and goes no further. The pass will be shut for about six days.
- **The detective:** James Flint, 26, Kingsport City Police. Three years as the department's photographer, promoted to detective two months ago. His boss, Detective Inspector Eleanor Kessler, 38, the youngest inspector Kingsport ever made, thinks his promotion was a mistake and gives him errands. Tonight's errand: carry a sealed evidence box over the pass to the county court. The label reads "Harrow County Court. Inquiry HM-66. Reopened." James doesn't know what that means yet. *"Try not to solve anything."*
- **The crime:** that night Northvale's photographer, Helen Graves, dies at the bottom of her darkroom stairs. It looks like a fall. It isn't.
- **The old secret:** in January 1966, nine men died in the Ashcroft family's Harrow Silver Mine. The town calls it an accident. Helen took the famous photograph of that night, and the morning after her death it is missing from her wall.
- **Why James gets involved:** the county coroner can't cross the pass, regulations say the scene must be photographed, and the only other person in town who knows a camera is the dead woman.
- **What the chapter is really about:** a young man proving his boss wrong, and a young woman pretending very hard that she hasn't fallen for him.
- **Chapter title:** *Developed in Silence* (James and Helen's apprentice develop the dead woman's last roll of film in the dark).

## Cast of Chapter 1

Ten people appear in Chapter 1, plus Gwen, the hotel receptionist (dry, unimpressed, a few lines). James, Mary and Katherine stay for the whole game. Mary and James are a slow burn across all seven chapters; Katherine becomes James's closest friend and partner, never a romance.

## The story, start to finish

**In one sentence: a young detective sent on an errand gets snowed in, meets two very different young women, and by the next night has proved a murder his boss told him to leave alone.**

**The errand.** James Flint, 26, two months a detective, sits on the last train over the Harrow Pass with a sealed evidence box on his knees. His boss, D.I. Eleanor Kessler, gave it to him with one instruction: try not to solve anything. Halfway up the mountain, snow nobody forecast buries the pass. The train stops at Northvale and goes no further. Arthur Bellamy, the conductor, is more upset that they arrived three minutes late.

**The Pass Hotel.** Mayor Victor Ashcroft gives every stranded passenger a free room, gives his own coat to old Bellamy, and hands James a key on a wooden pear the size of a fist. At the bar is Mary Hill, 23, fast-talking and teasing, in a lipstick called Fire Engine. She falls for him on the spot and spends the rest of the night pretending she hasn't. In the corner, Helen Graves, the town photographer, is photographing every stranded guest. Her shy apprentice Katherine comes to fetch her home and gets sent away. Helen tells James a photograph is the only witness that never changes its story. Then she stares at someone across the room and goes quiet.

**11:42.** The power line comes down and every light in Northvale goes out. Every electric clock stops. Bellamy, in the corridor with a candle, wants the time on record.

**Morning.** Ruby Hayes from the café bursts in: Helen is at the bottom of her darkroom stairs. Sergeant Walter Briggs, ex-army, the town's only policeman, has no use for city detectives, but the law says the scene must be photographed and nobody else can. He takes James, and tests him every step of the way.

**The studio.** It looks like a fall: steep stairs and a whisky bottle. But Ruby says Helen hadn't touched a drop in ten years, and the bottle is nearly full while the glass is wet. The stair post is freshly scraped, and Briggs found finger-shaped bruises on Helen's wrist. Two teacups sit on the table, one with bright red lipstick on the rim. The famous photograph of the 1966 mine disaster is missing from the wall. And there's a camera on a tripod, rigged to fire when the cellar door opens. Katherine arrives and refuses to leave: she set that trap up herself two days ago, because Helen was sure someone had been getting into the studio at night.

**The darkroom.** James and Katherine develop the last roll together by red light. Most frames are last night's bar: every stranded guest. The last frame is the cellar door, lit by the flash. Under the loupe: the electric clock on the wall stopped at 11:42, so it was taken after the blackout. The visitor's boots are dry, so they never went outside. From their pocket hangs a wooden pear: a Pass Hotel room key. And in their other hand is a gilt frame: the missing mine photograph.

**The phone call.** Eleanor Kessler gets through on the hotel line. Leave it alone, she says. Ashcroft plays golf with the Commissioner. The player decides how James answers. After she hangs up, the player sees what James can't: Kessler taking his personnel file out of her desk drawer.

**The lie.** Mary says she was behind the bar all night. Bellamy says she closed at 11:15. James shows her the lipstick teacup. She admits she went to Helen at half past eleven: Helen had something of her father's, but was frightened and sent her home at 11:40. Two minutes later the lights went out, and Helen's camera saw someone else come in.

**The choice.** Briggs asks James if there's anything else he should know. Tell him about Mary, or protect her. Either way, the board says the same thing: the fall was staged, the visitor came from inside the hotel for the mine photograph, and Helen Graves was murdered by someone sleeping under James's roof.

**The ending.** Candlelight in the bar. Mary tells James only that her father died in the mine, nearly admits something else, flicks him on the forehead and runs upstairs. In Room 9 the developed prints are gone, all except the one James kept in his jacket, and someone has tried to break into the evidence box. James finally reads its label properly: Inquiry HM-66. Harrow Mine, 1966. On the pillow, a note: THE PASS OPENS IN SIX DAYS. BE ON THE FIRST TRAIN.

## Full script

How to read it:

- **NAME:** is a spoken line. *(Italics)* are actions and stage notes. **JAMES (thinking):** is his inner voice.
- **CHOOSE** marks a moment where the player picks what James says. The tag after an option, like *(Mary +1)*, is a hidden bond that changes later lines and scenes.
- **QUIZ** marks a moment where someone tests James and the player must pick the right answer. Wrong answers cost nothing but pride.
- **Mary moment** and **Kessler moment** are short cut-aways the player sees and James doesn't.
- "Evidence:" marks something that goes into the notebook.

### Scene 1: The last train (night)

*(Black screen. White text, alone:)* Winter, 1986. The forecast promised a clear night over the Harrow Pass.

*(The pass, clear and starlit. Then the snow arrives. The title. Then a train carriage: snow hammering the window. James, alone, a sealed evidence box on his knees. The train shudders and stops.)*

**JAMES (thinking):** The box hasn't moved in two hours. Neither has anything else.

**KESSLER (a memory, voice only):** It's a box, Flint. You carry it over the pass, you hand it to the county clerk, you come home. Try not to solve anything.

**JAMES (thinking):** Try not to solve anything. Two months a detective. Two months of carrying boxes. At this rate I'll make inspector by the time I'm a hundred and four.

*(Arthur Bellamy opens the carriage door, snow on his cap, pocket watch in hand.)*

**BELLAMY:** Northvale! Everybody off, please. The pass is closed.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Closed? It's a mountain. It doesn't have opening hours."*
   - **BELLAMY:** It does tonight, sir. Snow came over the top like someone tipped a bucket. Not a word of it in the forecast.
2. *"How long?"*
   - **BELLAMY:** Two days, if God is kind. A week, if He's busy.
3. *"Wake me when it's open."*
   - **BELLAMY:** The heating goes off in ten minutes, sir. You'll be awake. Very awake.

*(Any choice, then:)*

**BELLAMY:** *(checks his watch, pained)* We arrived at twenty-one seventeen. Three minutes late. I shall carry that to my grave.

**JAMES:** Three minutes isn't late, Mr...?

**BELLAMY:** Bellamy. And three minutes is how empires fall, sir.

*(The player walks James along the platform.)*

- **Payphone:** **JAMES:** Dead. *(beat)* Good. Nobody to tell me no.
- **Station clock:** **JAMES:** Electric. Twenty past nine. Make a note of it, James. Clocks in small towns know everything.
- **The evidence box:** **JAMES:** "Harrow County Court. Inquiry HM-66. Reopened." Sealed, labelled, boring. Kessler's way of saying "I believe in you" without believing in me.

### Scene 2: The Pass Hotel (lobby and bar)

*(Warm lamps, wet coats, a log fire. Stranded passengers everywhere. Victor Ashcroft at the desk, silver hair, perfect coat.)*

**ASHCROFT:** Welcome to Northvale. Snowed in, but never left out in the cold. That's what we say here.

**JAMES:** Since when?

**ASHCROFT:** Since about four minutes ago. *(smiles a beat too long)* Victor Ashcroft. I own the hotel, so the room is free. Room nine.

*(He hands James a key on a wooden pear the size of a fist.)*

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Is this a key or a weapon?"*
   - **ASHCROFT:** Guests kept walking off with the keys. Nobody walks off with that.
2. *"Very generous. What's the catch?"*
   - **ASHCROFT:** *(laughs warmly)* A detective. I can always tell. No catch, young man. When the pass closes, Northvale looks after its own, and tonight you're our own.
3. *"Thank you, Mr Ashcroft."*
   - **ASHCROFT:** Victor, please. Anything you need, ask Mary at the bar. Within reason.

*(Ashcroft's eyes rest on the evidence box under James's arm for one second. Then Bellamy shuffles in behind James, shivering in a thin uniform coat. Ashcroft takes off his own coat, charcoal wool with a curly black astrakhan collar, and puts it round the old man's shoulders without a word.)*

**BELLAMY:** Sir, I couldn't possibly...

**ASHCROFT:** You can and you will. *(to the room, raising his voice)* Everyone stuck here tonight eats on my account. No arguments. *(to Bellamy, quieter)* I hear you were three minutes late. Let me make it up to the railway.

**BELLAMY:** *(close to tears)* ...Sir.

**JAMES (thinking):** A coat that cost more than my flat. Either the nicest man in the mountains, or very good at looking like it. I hope it's the first. I really do.

**At the bar: Mary Hill**

*(Mary has her back to the room, stacking glasses, humming. She turns. Sees James. A glass slips. She catches it an inch from the floor.)*

**MARY:** ...I meant to do that.

**JAMES:** Of course you did.

**MARY:** You look like a man who's just been told his train isn't going anywhere.

**JAMES:** And you look like a woman who's told eleven men that tonight.

**MARY:** Twelve. You're the first one who hasn't cried. *(beat)* Yet.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Give me time. The night's young."* *(Mary +1)*
   - **MARY:** *(trying not to smile, failing)* Coffee, then. For the crying.
2. *"I don't cry. I'm a detective."*
   - **MARY:** Detectives cry. They just do it in the car.
3. *"Is that lipstick legal in a snowstorm?"* *(Mary +1)*
   - **MARY:** It's called Fire Engine. Arrest me.
   - **JAMES:** Tempting.
   - **MARY:** *(the glass slips again. She catches it again.)* ...Coffee. You're having coffee.

*(Then, whatever was chosen:)*

**MARY:** Fire Engine, by the way. The lipstick. In case you were wondering. Which you weren't. Obviously.

**JAMES (thinking):** Bright red, the brave kind. Remember that, James. You'll want to.

Topics:

1. *Are you always this cheerful in a blizzard?*
   - **MARY:** I'm from here. Blizzards are the only thing in Northvale that arrive on time.
   - **JAMES:** Tell that to the conductor.
   - **MARY:** Mr Bellamy? He's been in twice to complain my clock is two minutes fast. Offered to fix it. Twice. I said I like living in the future.
2. *What does a person do for fun in Northvale?*
   - **MARY:** Leave.
   - **JAMES:** And you didn't.
   - **MARY:** I got a passport once. Helen took the photo. *(beat)* It's in a drawer. It's very well travelled. It's been to the drawer and back.
3. *Who's the photographer in the corner?*
   - **MARY:** Helen Graves. She's photographed every wedding, christening and scraped knee in this town since she was sixteen. She's the best person I know. Be nice to her or I'll water your coffee.
4. *Goodnight, Mary.*
   - **MARY:** Goodnight, Detective. *(beat)* Not that I care whether it's good. It's a figure of speech.

**Mary moment** *(after James walks away)*

*(Mary alone behind the bar. She puts her forehead on the counter.)*

**MARY:** "I meant to do that." You absolute... *(sits up, points at her own reflection in a glass)* No. Absolutely not. He's a city cop, he's leaving in six days, and he has a stupid face.

*(beat)*

**MARY:** ...A very stupid face.

**Helen Graves** *(corner table, a cup of tea, camera in her lap)*

*(Flash. James blinks.)*

**HELEN:** Hold still. There. Sorry. Everyone stuck in one room on one night. I couldn't resist.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"You could've asked. I'd have done my good side."*
   - **HELEN:** Young man, at your age they're all good sides. Wait till you're fifty-four.
2. *"Nice camera. Old Leica?"* *(Helen warms up)*
   - **HELEN:** *(delighted)* Someone who knows! Older than you, and twice as reliable.
3. *"Do you always photograph strangers?"*
   - **HELEN:** Strangers are just townsfolk who haven't been here long enough.

**HELEN:** You've used a camera for work. I can tell from how you looked at the flash. You didn't blink, you checked where it came from.

**JAMES:** Three years photographing crime scenes. Now I'm a detective.

**HELEN:** Then you know. A photograph is the only witness that never changes its story. *(she looks around the room, slowly)* One day, somebody will want to know exactly who was in this room tonight.

**JAMES:** Why would anyone want that?

*(Helen's eyes settle on someone across the bar. The player can't see who. She puts the lens cap on.)*

**HELEN:** Drink your coffee, Detective. It's going to be a long winter.

*(A young woman in a snowy coat stops at the table. Dark hair, very still, very pretty, holding an umbrella that is no use at all.)*

**KATHERINE:** Helen. It's nine fifty. You said nine.

**HELEN:** Go home, Kat. I'll finish the roll. *(to James)* My apprentice. Katherine. She thinks I'm going to fall asleep in a bar.

**KATHERINE:** You fell asleep in a bar in March.

**HELEN:** That was a café.

**KATHERINE:** It had a bar.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Nice umbrella. Very optimistic."*
   - **KATHERINE:** *(looks at it)* It's for the walk home. Snow is mostly water.
2. *"James Flint. Pleasure."* *(Katherine +1)*
   - **KATHERINE:** Katherine Alvarez. *(pause)* I don't know if it's a pleasure yet. I'll tell you.
3. *"Does she always work this late?"*
   - **KATHERINE:** Yes. That's why I come. *(pause)* Goodnight.

*(Katherine leaves. Helen watches her go with real fondness.)*

**HELEN:** She'll be a better photographer than me. She just doesn't know it yet. *(beat)* Goodnight, Detective.

**Desmond Fairweather** *(optional, by the fire, guarding a large box)*

**FAIRWEATHER:** You look like a man with carpets.

**JAMES:** I look like a man with a train that isn't moving.

**FAIRWEATHER:** The Tornado 3000. Twin brushes, three speeds, the sound of a jet taking off. Dust fears it.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"I'll take two."*
   - **FAIRWEATHER:** *(near tears)* Sir. Nobody has ever said that to me before.
   - **JAMES:** I was joking.
   - **FAIRWEATHER:** I know. Let me have this.
2. *"We're in a blizzard."*
   - **FAIRWEATHER:** Dust doesn't stop for weather, Detective. Neither do I.

**Mary moment** *(if the player talked to Katherine)*

*(Mary, polishing a glass, watching James talk to Katherine. She polishes harder.)*

**MARY:** Katherine Alvarez. Of course. Quiet, mysterious, pretty, *boring*... *(the glass squeaks)* Not that I care.

### Scene 3: 11:42 (Room 9 and the corridor)

*(Room 9. A narrow bed, a window full of snow.)*

- **Window:** **JAMES:** Six days of this. Kessler's going to think I did it on purpose.
- **Bed:** **JAMES:** Hospital corners. Tidier than my entire life.
- **The evidence box:** **JAMES:** Still sealed. Still boring. Goodnight, box.

*(The lights die. All of them, at once. Silence. Then a candle in the corridor.)*

**JAMES (thinking):** At eleven forty-two the lights went out. The whole town at once, like somebody shutting a book.

**BELLAMY:** *(in the doorway, candle, nightcap)* Eleven forty-two! Exactly. I had just wound my watch, Detective. I want that noted somewhere.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Noted. In my heart."*
   - **BELLAMY:** *(touched)* That's the best place for it, sir.
2. *"Every electric clock in town just stopped, didn't it?"*
   - **BELLAMY:** *(quietly thrilled)* Every single one. For once, the whole town agrees on the time.
   - **JAMES (thinking):** He's right. Frozen at 11:42, all of them. That's not a power cut. That's a timestamp.

### Scene 4: Morning (the hotel lobby)

*(Grey light. Power still out. Bellamy, swamped in Ashcroft's big coat, glares at the dead lobby clock. The front door bangs open. Snow and Ruby Hayes come in together: red coat, dark curls, mascara running.)*

**RUBY:** Walter! Somebody get Walter! Helen is at the bottom of her stairs and she won't wake up!

*(Sergeant Walter Briggs stands up from a lobby armchair: broad, grey crew cut, a cat on his shoulder.)*

**BRIGGS:** Ruby. Sit. Breathe. *(to the cat)* Corporal, down. *(the cat stays)* ...Fine.

*(Briggs sees James.)*

**BRIGGS:** You. City. Ashcroft says you're a detective.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"The best one in this hotel."*
   - **BRIGGS:** You're the only one in this hotel.
   - **JAMES:** And yet, still the best.
   - **BRIGGS:** *(stares)* ...God help us.
2. *"Detective Flint, Kingsport. How can I help, Sergeant?"* *(Briggs +1)*
   - **BRIGGS:** Manners. Good. Keep them. You'll need them.
3. *"Technically I'm a courier this week."*
   - **BRIGGS:** Then technically you can carry a camera.

**BRIGGS:** Coroner can't cross the pass. Regulations say a sudden death gets photographed. Only other person in Northvale who knows one end of a camera from the other is... *(he stops)* ...is the one at the bottom of the stairs.

**RUBY:** *(wiping her eyes, looking James up and down)* You're the photographer? *(beat)* God, they're making detectives pretty in the city.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Only the clever ones."*
   - **RUBY:** Careful, Detective. I eat clever boys for breakfast. *(her voice cracks)* ...I brought her breakfast. Every morning.
2. *"I'm sorry about your friend."* *(Ruby +1)*
   - **RUBY:** *(stops, softens)* ...Thank you, love. You're kind. Don't let it go to your head.

**BRIGGS:** You do what I say, you touch what I say, and you keep your city mouth shut in front of the townsfolk. Understood?

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Yes, Sergeant."* *(Briggs +1)*
2. *"Mostly understood."*
   - **BRIGGS:** Mostly is how men lose fingers, kid.

**JAMES:** *(picks up his coat)* Lead the way.

### Scene 5: Graves Studio (the crime scene)

*(A narrow shop: "GRAVES STUDIO. Portraits, Weddings, Passports. Since 1948." Cold. A whole wall of framed photographs. Stairs down to the darkroom. At the bottom, a shape under a white sheet. We never see Helen.)*

**BRIGGS:** I covered her. Regulations can lump it.

**JAMES:** No. That was right.

*(James stands at the top of the stairs and looks down at the sheet. For the first time in the game there is no joke on his face at all. The music drops to almost nothing.)*

**JAMES (thinking):** Last night she told me to drink my coffee. She had tea with three sugars and a camera in her lap, and she was scared, and I didn't ask why.

**JAMES (thinking):** ...Alright, Helen. I'm asking now.

**BRIGGS:** *(a long look at James)* Hm. Camera's yours. Twelve frames. Make them count.

*(Film counter on screen: 12. Photographing a hotspot uses one frame. Evidence comes from what James photographs and what he notices. There are more than 12 things to look at, so the player chooses. Photographing the sheet is the one thing Briggs stops: "Have some respect, kid." No frame is used.)*

- **The stairs:**
  - **JAMES:** Steep. Bad light. Good place for an accident. That's the problem. It's too good.
  - **JAMES:** Her reading glasses are folded in her cardigan pocket. People who fall down stairs don't stop to fold their glasses first.
  - Evidence: **Folded glasses**
- **The stair post** *(the heavy wooden post at the top of the stairs)*:
  - **JAMES:** Fresh scrapes in the varnish. Pale wood showing. Something hit this hard, last night, at about the height of a shoulder.
  - **JAMES (thinking):** People who trip fall forward. People who are pushed hit things on the way.
  - Evidence: **Scraped stair post** *(must be photographed)*
- **Briggs, about the body** *(after the stair post)*:
  - **BRIGGS:** *(quietly, so Ruby won't hear)* When I covered her. Her left wrist. Four bruises, in a row, and one on the other side.
  - **JAMES:** Fingers and a thumb.
  - **BRIGGS:** Somebody held her by the arm. Hard. *(beat)* I was a soldier twenty years, kid. I know what a grip looks like.
  - Evidence: **Bruises on Helen's wrist** *(Briggs's statement)*
- **Whisky bottle and glass** *(on the shelf by the stairs)*:
  - **JAMES:** Whisky. Seal broken. Nearly full. Glass beside it, wet.
  - **QUIZ** *(Briggs)*: "Nearly full bottle, wet glass. What's it tell you, Detective?"
    1. *She had one drink and slipped.* → **BRIGGS:** That's what it looks like. I asked what it tells you.
    2. *Someone poured a drink and nobody drank it.* *(Briggs +1)* → **BRIGGS:** *(grunts)* ...Keep going.
    3. *She was a very tidy drinker.* → **BRIGGS:** Was that a joke? At a scene? *(beat)* Don't.
  - Evidence: **The whisky bottle**
- **Ruby** *(by the door, still holding a breakfast tray)*:
  - **RUBY:** Eggs, toast, and a cinnamon bun she pretended not to want. Ten years. *(beat)* Now what do I do with the bun?
  - **CHOOSE**
    1. *"Did she drink?"*
    2. *"Give me the bun. I'll grieve with it."* → **RUBY:** *(laughs through tears)* You're terrible. *(hands it over)* Thank you. *(Ruby +1)*
  - **RUBY:** Helen? Not a drop in ten years, not since her Harold passed. Tea, three sugars. I'd have smelled whisky on her from across the street. I run a café in a mining town, love. I know what every soul here drinks and who they lie to about it.
  - Evidence: **Ruby: "Sober ten years"**
  - *(If spoken to again:)* **RUBY:** Stop looking at me like that, Detective. I'm grieving. *(beat)* Look at me like that later.
- **Two teacups** *(on the little table by the counter)*:
  - **JAMES:** Two cups. She had company last night.
  - **JAMES:** And her company left a mark on the rim. Bright red. The brave kind.
  - **JAMES (thinking):** ...Fire Engine. Oh, Mary. What did you do?
  - Evidence: **Lipstick on a teacup**
- **The wall of photographs:**
  - **JAMES:** Forty years of Northvale. Weddings, miners, a brass band, a dog wearing a hat. She had a good eye.
  - **JAMES:** And one gap. A clean square on the wallpaper. The brass plate's still here: *Harrow Mine, January 1966.*
  - **BRIGGS:** The mine photograph. Nine men died down there. Ashcroft's mine. Helen took that picture for the paper. It's hung there twenty years.
  - Evidence: **The missing frame**
- **The camera on the tripod:**
  - **JAMES:** A camera on a tripod, aimed at the cellar door. Black thread from the shutter release to the door handle.
  - **QUIZ** *(Briggs)*: "Why rig a camera to a cellar door?"
    1. *To photograph the cat.* → **BRIGGS:** Corporal doesn't use doors.
    2. *She expected someone to come in that way, at night.* *(Briggs +1)* → **BRIGGS:** *(quietly)* ...She was scared.
    3. *An art project.* → **BRIGGS:** Kid.
  - **JAMES:** The counter says the roll's finished. Twenty-four frames, still inside.
  - Evidence: **Helen's last roll of film**
- **The cellar door:**
  - **BRIGGS:** Old coal cellar. Locked for years. Nobody's got a key.
  - **JAMES:** Somebody's got a reason, then.

**Katherine arrives** *(after the player has looked at the camera)*

*(The bell over the door. Katherine, snow in her hair, out of breath. She sees the sheet. She stops completely still.)*

**BRIGGS:** Alvarez. Go home.

**KATHERINE:** No.

**BRIGGS:** That's an order.

**KATHERINE:** I'm not in your army, Sergeant. *(her voice doesn't shake, her hands do)* And this is my darkroom now. She told me so in March. I wrote it down.

**CHOOSE**

1. *"Let her stay. I need someone who knows this room."* *(Katherine +1)*
   - **BRIGGS:** *(long breath)* ...Fine. She touches nothing.
   - **KATHERINE:** I touch everything. That's what apprentices do.

## Where it goes after Chapter 1, and decisions

**The full game covers six snowed-in days: one murder, one buried mine disaster, a mayor who is not what he seems, James and Mary falling for each other while neither fully trusts the other, and a boss in Kingsport who slowly stops doubting him.**

**Ashcroft's seeds in Chapter 1** (small, deniable, all pay off in Chapters 6 and 7):

- Helen stares at someone across the bar and stops talking. In Chapter 6 the player learns it was Ashcroft.
- His eyes rest on James's evidence box for one second at check-in.
- The missing photograph is of his family's mine. He owns the hotel, so he could get any room key, and his own master key hangs on the same wooden pear.
- His coat for Bellamy: charcoal wool, astrakhan collar. In Chapter 2, Katherine's enlargement of frame 24 shows that collar on the visitor.
- A fresh plaster on the back of his right hand, "chipping ice off the front steps". In Chapter 6, Gwen says the porter does the steps.
- Kessler warns James: "He plays golf with the Commissioner." Ashcroft's reach goes all the way to Kingsport.

**Eleanor Kessler across the game** (one phone call or cut-away per chapter, never a romance):
