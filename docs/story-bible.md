# Below the Pass: Story Bible (approved canon)

Approved 2026-11-14. This is the **true story** behind the mystery. Every script, milestone doc and chapter design builds on it. If a script line disagrees with this file, the script is wrong, or this file must be updated on purpose first.

**Labels**

## 1. The truth in one paragraph

In January 1966, nine men died in the Harrow Mine because the emergency gate was chained shut with a company padlock. Helen Graves photographed the disaster. The famous published photograph was cropped, and her full print shows the chained gate at its edge. She hung that full print on her studio wall for twenty years, where nobody looked closely. In 1986 she gave a sworn statement to the reopened inquiry, HM-66. Victor Ashcroft (mayor, hotel owner and Helen's landlord) learned of it from the Commissioner. On the night the snow closed the pass, he took the print. When he came back for the negative, he killed her and staged it as a drunk's fall. Her trap camera photographed him.

## 2. Core facts

1. **Graves Studio is Victor's building.** It was the old Ashcroft mining company office, and Helen has rented it for decades **[Later, Ch2]**. She lives in two rooms above the shop **[Later, Ch2]**.
2. **The coal passage.** Under the studio, an old coal cellar connects through a passage to the Pass Hotel's cellar, beside the kitchen **[Later, Ch2]**. The cellar door in the studio is "locked for years. Nobody's got a key" **[Ch1, Briggs]**. Nobody except the landlord.
3. **The print.** The framed mine photograph on Helen's wall was her full, uncropped print. The chained gate is visible only under a loupe **[Later, Ch3]**.
4. **The negative.** Helen hid the 1966 negative somewhere else. It was "something of your dad's" that she meant to give Mary **[Ch1, Mary; full reveal Ch3–4]**. Its exact hiding place will be designed in Chapter 3, and should be tied to Mary.
5. **HM-66.** James's box holds the reopened inquiry's 1966 file, including Helen's sworn statement. It does **not** contain the photograph or the negative: Helen kept those back to give to Mary first **[Later, Ch4]**.
6. **Harold's bottle.** The whisky was the unopened bottle Helen's late husband Harold left. She kept it on the shelf by the stairs for ten years as her reminder **[Ch1: Ruby, "not since her Harold passed"; the bottle is Harold's: Later, Ch2]**. Staging her as a drunk discredits her sworn statement.
7. **Victor's master key** hangs on the same ugly wooden pear as the guest keys. It's the "hotel key in the pocket" in frame 24 **[Ch1 clue; owner's master key: Later, Ch6]**.

## 3. The trap camera (how it works)

- Katherine built it on Tuesday, because someone had been getting into the studio at night **[Ch1]**.
- Helen's camera is fully mechanical: the shutter is spring-driven and needs no electricity.
- A cable release is screwed into the shutter button. Black thread runs from its plunger to the cellar door handle. When the door opens, the thread pulls, the shutter fires, and the camera fires the flashgun through its flash contact **[Ch1]**.
- The flashgun is battery-powered: the same one Helen used all night at the bar **[Ch1: "Flash. James blinks."]**. The blackout can't stop it **[Seed: Katherine's line]**.
- Focus and aperture are preset for the doorway. The tripod stands across the room, facing the door.
- It fires once. The film must be wound on by hand before it can fire again **[Ch1]**.
- Helen used the same camera for work and at the bar, so she armed the trap **each night when she got home** **[Later, Ch2, one Katherine line]**.

## 6. Victor after the flash: dazzled, not blind

A flashgun fired a metre away in total darkness leaves a large purple blotch in the middle of a person's sight for about a minute. Sight at the edges comes back within seconds. His dark-adapted night vision is ruined for longer. So:
- **He can act.** He can move toward a voice, grab and shove using the edges of his sight, and he has walked this room in the dark for weeks.
- **He can stage by touch.** The bottle is on the shelf right by the stairs, where he knows it is.
- **He can't search.** Finding one negative in a darkroom full of them needs light. In a blacked-out street, even a match in the shop window would be the only light in Northvale.
- **He never finds the camera.** He believes Helen fired a flash at him by hand, as she did all night at the bar. The tripod stands across the room, away from his path between the door, the stairs and the shelf. Briggs's torch ends any search.

## 7. The coat (the misdirection)

**Why Bellamy looks guilty:** he wore the coat in public before and after the murder; he was awake at 11:42 and knows exact times; and he asked Helen about the mine photograph at the bar, which shows in one of her frames **[Later, Ch2]**.

**Why he's cleared:** he was in the lobby from 11:45 pm to 12:30 am, writing timed letters, and Gwen confirms it. He never left the hotel, so he can't explain coal dust **[Later, Ch2]**.

## 8. Room 9 and the evidence box (evening, day 1)

**What Victor wants, in order:**
1. **The prints.** Was he identified? He takes every print. On a normal-size print there's no face, and the collar is a dark smudge. He relaxes too soon: only Katherine's bigger enlargement from the negatives shows the collar. Katherine keeps the negatives, and in Chapter 2 Victor learns this, which puts her at risk.
2. **The box.** He knows exactly what HM-66 is: the inquiry file with Helen's statement. His one urgent question is whether Helen sent the photograph or the negative to the inquiry with her statement. If she did, it's in this box and he must destroy it. If not, the proof is still in Northvale and he can keep hunting.

**How he gets in:**
- **The room:** the owner's master key, on its pear. No force; James finds his door locked as he left it **[Ch1; master key: Later, Ch6]**.
- **The box:** a steel police box with a sealed lock. No hotel key opens it. He turns out James's coat pockets looking for its key **[Ch1]**, finds none, and attacks the seal with a hotel letter-opener. He fails **[Ch1]**.

**Why the note, "THE PASS OPENS IN SIX DAYS. BE ON THE FIRST TRAIN.":** He can't open the box. If the proof is inside, the inquiry already has it and nothing he does tonight changes that. What he can still control is the one person investigating Helen's death. If James leaves, the death stays an "accident", her statement becomes a drunk's story, and the negative stays hidden in Northvale for Victor to find.

**When:** while James sits with Mary in the candlelit bar, which is Victor's own bar **[Ch1, Scene 11]**.

## 9. Proving Victor killed Helen, not just visited

The final accusation must not depend on a confession. The player builds five separate strands:

1. **Timing.** Helen was alive at 11:40 (Mary). The visitor came through the door after 11:42 (frame 24). Nobody else came in: the cellar door was locked again behind him **[Ch1]**, and Ruby had to let herself in with her own key in the morning because the front door was locked from inside **[Later, Ch2, one Ruby line]**.
2. **Identity.** Board links A–D: the coat, the dust, the passage prints, the landlord key and the master key on a pear.
3. **Violence.** The wrist bruises (four in a row and a thumb opposite: a right hand gripping her left wrist, a man's span) **[Ch1]** and the stair post at shoulder height **[Ch1]** prove a grab and a shove, not a fall. Victor's plaster on his right hand **[Seed]**, explained as "chipping ice off the front steps", collapses in Chapter 6 when Gwen says the porter does the steps and Mr Ashcroft hasn't held a shovel in twenty years. When the pass opens, the coroner finds skin under Helen's fingernails **[Later, Ch7]**.
4. **Guilty knowledge.** The staging used Harold's bottle, which only someone who knew the studio by night would know about. Innocent people don't stage accidents. Add the Room 9 break-in, the note and the false alibi.
5. **The frame.** In Chapter 6 the player finds Helen's empty gilt frame (beaded edge, one of six made in 1966 **[Ch1, Katherine]**, "Graves Studio" label on the back) at the bottom of the coal chute in Victor's passage **[Later, Ch6]**. It's the frame the visitor holds in frame 24.

Confession is optional. It's a reward for a strong accusation, never the proof.

## 10. Eleanor Kessler's contradiction (deliberate)

On the phone she orders James off the case, while privately she wants him to succeed. Four layers explain it:
1. **She can't speak freely.** The call runs through Ashcroft's hotel switchboard **[Later, Ch4]**. "Stay in the hotel, touch nothing" is the only safe thing to say on that line.
2. **She's afraid for him.** A rookie alone in Ashcroft's town.
3. **She's protecting his career.** On record, she must have ordered him off.
4. **She's testing him.** Telling James Flint not to do something is the surest way to get it done. The Chapter 1 cut-away shows it **[Ch1]**.

The payoff: in Chapter 4 she chose him because nobody watches the boy who carries boxes. In Chapter 7 she admits she was wrong about him, and it costs her. Never a romance.
